## Goal
Typing an ingredient offers a real dictionary entry for almost anything a home cook writes, in Russian or English, so recipes are found by ingredient, scaled whole where they must be, compared by amount, and tagged from a real tag list.

## What exists at the start
On `develop` @ 5646333 (v0.13 at demo). The shipped dictionary has five ingredients (курица, крылышки, мясо, фарш, говядина: ids 1–5) and two tags (итальянская, грузинская: ids 10, 11). Entries carry `divisible`, `factors` (g, ml, pc, tsp, tbsp, cup, pinch) and `removed` (v0.12). The snapshot is read once per installation and benched at 5 000 entries (v0.12). The asset's `version` is a SHA-256 the app checks; it was typed by hand-run tooling until now.

## Demo scenarios
1. In the editor type "мац", "лук", "чесн", "сливоч", "parm": each offers the right entry (мацони; лук репчатый and its kin; чеснок / зубчик чеснока; сливочное масло, сливки; parmesan → пармезан), leaves before parents.
2. Write a recipe with 3 яйца and 2 зубчика чеснока in шт: no divisibility question; 1½ eggs is marked whole pieces only; 150 g мука asks nothing either.
3. Filter the book by "мука" or "сахар": recipes holding it in g, cups or spoons are ordered by how much per serving.
4. Tags offer real cuisines, meal types, diets and methods (e.g. "аэрогриль", "выпечка", "завтрак", "вегетарианское").
5. The owner's lemon-cake recipe, typed again in the editor, finds an entry for every ingredient except perhaps the most unusual one.

## Out of scope
- Synonyms and alternative spellings beyond what the search folding already does (ё/е, case); plural forms as separate entries.
- Moving existing recipes' free-text ingredients onto entries (no migration; the owner's book holds test data).
- A third language.

## Dictionary content rules
- **Labels.** Short base nominative forms an author would type: Russian as cooks write it in a list ("лук репчатый", "мука пшеничная", "яйцо куриное" or simply "яйцо" with leaves); English likewise. Russian is the primary language of the product; English labels are natural, not transliterations.
- **Families, two levels.** A generic top-level entry with its kinds as leaves (мука → мука пшеничная, мука ржаная…; сыр → пармезан, моцарелла…). A leaf is never a parent. An entry that is not a kind of anything is top-level with no leaves.
- **What a piece is.** A `pc` factor is the weight of the thing cooks count under that label (one egg, one onion, one clove). Where cooks count different things under one word, make separate entries (чеснок — a head; зубчик чеснока — a clove).
- **Divisibility.** `divisible: false` for what cannot be split in a recipe (an egg, a clove, a bay leaf); `true` for what can be used in halves (an onion, a lemon, a pepper); every entry with a `pc` factor has the flag.
- **Factors, one scale.** Grams per unit for every entry: g = 1; ml = grams per millilitre (density); pc = grams per piece; tsp, tbsp, cup (a cup is 250 ml) and pinch = grams each, level measure. Give only the factors a cook would plausibly use for that ingredient; never guess wildly — use common culinary references.
- **Ids.** Each family source owns a range (T-119 fixes them; e.g. produce 1 000–2 999, animal 3 000–4 999, pantry 5 000–6 999, seasoning and the rest 7 000–8 999, tags 100–999; 1–5 and 10–11 stay). Ids are permanent once shipped.
- **No brands**, no recipes-as-ingredients ("тесто для пиццы" is fine as a bought product; "соус бешамель" as a sauce is fine), no duplicates across families (the generator refuses duplicate labels).
