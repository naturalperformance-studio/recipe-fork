# v0.32 — A shopping list made from recipes
**Status:** active
**Planned:** 2026-10-12 · **Shipped:** —

Chosen by the owner on 2026-10-12 after v0.31 shipped, over printing/PDF (FLOW-075, next) and more interface work. Builds FLOW-074 (`spec/70-flows/75-shopping-and-printing.md`), with two additions the owner asked for at planning: one entry in different units merges approximately, and the reader adds lines of their own (equipment included).

## Goal
From one or several recipes, each at its own serving count, the reader gets one shopping list on the phone — the same ingredient added up even across units, their own lines beside it — and ticks off what is bought.

## Demo scenarios
1. Open a recipe, set 6 servings, ⋮ → "Add to shopping list" → a notice "Added" with "Open"; the list holds the six-serving quantities.
2. Add a second recipe that also uses onions → onions are one line with the sum; 300 g of flour and 1.2 kg of flour are one line "1.5 kg"; under each line, muted, the recipes it comes from.
3. Flour in grams in one recipe and in cups in another → one line marked "≈" (e.g. "≈ 560 g") with a muted note of what was counted approximately ("2 cups counted as ≈ 260 g"); an entry with no known measure for a unit keeps that part as its own line. Free-text ingredients stay their own lines as written; "salt — to taste" from two recipes is one line, and a measured salt line gains "+ to taste".
4. On the list, add a line of your own — typing offers ingredients and equipment from the dictionaries ("baking paper", "tart tin 24 cm", "batteries") → it appears in the list and can be ticked and removed like any line.
5. Tap a line → it is struck through and moves to "In the basket" at the bottom; marks survive a restart; adding a recipe that raises a ticked line's amount unticks it.
6. The list opens from an icon in the book's top bar; its recipes are listed at the top, each with the card's servings −/+ — set 4 servings there and every line it feeds changes at once; a recipe can be removed (with Undo), and adding it again from its card updates its servings rather than doubling it; "Clear list" offers Undo. An empty list says how to add a recipe.

## Out of scope
- Sending or sharing the list anywhere (FLOW-074: the list is sent nowhere, RET-010).
- US units and any conversion beyond the dictionary's measures for one entry.
- What is already at home (no stock-keeping); recipe equipment is not added automatically — the reader adds what they need as their own line.
- Several lists, or lists per book.

### T-228 — The rule that adds up a shopping list
**Status:** done
**Serves:** 2, 3 · **Core:** yes · **Depends on:** —
**Expected files:** `core/…/shopping/` (new: the contributions, the merge, the line model), tests in `core/src/test`.
**Criteria:**
- Input: recipes, each with its ingredient rows, authored servings and the requested serving count, plus each dictionary entry's measures (its comparison factors, grams per unit). Output: lines in a stable order (the order of first appearance), each with its name source (entry or free text), its quantity and unit or "to taste", whether it is approximate, what was counted approximately, and the recipes it comes from.
- Each recipe's rows are scaled exactly as the card scales them (`ScaledRecipe`), summed as exact fractions, and rounded once, on the grid of the line's unit, when shown; exact units add in their base unit and show on the magnitude ladder (300 g + 1.2 kg → 1.5 kg).
- One entry's rows in one unit type (or one household unit) add exactly. One entry's rows in different units merge into one line in the unit of its largest part (by the entry's measures), each other part converted by the entry's measures; such a line is approximate and names each converted part. A part whose unit the entry has no measure for stays its own line.
- To-taste rows of one entry are one "to taste" line, or, where the entry also has a measured line, mark that line "+ to taste". Free-text rows are each their own line as written. Rows that cannot be scaled (incomplete, off-grid) are their own lines as written. Qualifiers do not split an entry's line.
- Adding the same recipe twice is the caller's to avoid; the rule takes each contribution as given.
**Notes:** Review 1 (mutation: every criterion's test failed when its behaviour was removed): fix — must: a converted part in the approximate note was raised to its grid's floor while the total added the exact value (1 cup + 1 tbsp → "≈ 1 cup", note "1 tbsp ≈ ¼ cup"). Nits taken: the qualifier test pins "not approximate"; the "halves where any row is divisible" rule tested; a DOM-018 row's list value pinned. Fix round 1: a part below the line grid's floor is noted "< ¼ cup". Review 2 (all five probes now fail tests): pass; merged 2026-10-12. Not taken: notes above the floor need not add up to the line (≈); `ShoppingRecipe` does not itself refuse a count ≤ 0 (callers hold the card's ¼ floor).

### T-231 — Ticking off what is bought
**Status:** done
**Serves:** 5 · **Core:** no · **Depends on:** T-229
**Expected files:** `shopping/` (the screen's lines and the store's ticks), tests.
**Criteria:**
- A tap on a line ticks it: struck through, read as done by the screen reader (toggleable, checkbox role), moved to an "In the basket" section at the bottom; a second tap unticks it. Ticks survive leaving the app and a restart.
- A ticked line whose amount grows (a recipe added or its servings raised) is unticked; one whose amount shrinks or stays keeps its tick; a line that disappears drops its tick.
- "Clear ticked" in the ⋮ removes the ticked own lines and the ticks (offers Undo); recipes stay.
**Notes:** Review 1: fix — musts: two guards that keep ticks were untested (settling waits for every listed recipe to be read; settling waits for the dictionary — a cold start where recipes come first would drop every entry tick). Nits taken: toggle on the shown state; never settle against an unread dictionary's fallback; one factor filter for keys and sums; collision-proof line keys. Fix round 1 done. Review 2 (each guard removed → its test failed): pass; merged 2026-10-12. Not taken: a tap while the dictionary cannot be read stores a key that is settled away once it reads; ticks are settled only while the list is open (shown against the current amount anyway); an as-written row's tick goes when its note or qualifier is edited.
