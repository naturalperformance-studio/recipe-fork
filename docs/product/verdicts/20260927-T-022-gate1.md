**Failed check:** C3 — the task carries a criterion whose *Then* constrains a behaviour the task does not build, and the behaviour it does build is narrower than the requirement states.
FLOW-013 (7) reads: "Given a running timer, when the reader cancels it, then nothing
announces itself afterwards." "Announces itself" is FLOW-013's own term for what a timer
does when it ends — its statement ("the system shall announce it whether the application
is in the foreground, in the background, or closed") and criteria (1)–(5). The entry's
carried-elsewhere line places FLOW-013 (1–5) at T-023, and T-023's entry places (6–7)
back here. T-022 therefore builds no announcement of any kind, and nothing in its
criteria says what a timer does when it ends.

As sliced, gate 2 cannot tell a right build from a wrong one. In T-022 nothing can
announce, so FLOW-013 (7) is met by every build, including one whose "cancel" only hides
the countdown while the timer's end stays scheduled. An announcement that the build
added to make the criterion observable would be behaviour none of T-022's criteria asks
for and would meet the gate-2 sweep as an addition. When T-023 then builds the
announcement, it does not carry FLOW-013 (7), so a cancelled timer that still announces
itself is walked by no criterion at any gate: the cancellation the requirement states —
an act that stops the announcement — is gated nowhere.

The reading under which the task would pass — that a test in T-022 can observe
"nothing announces" by asserting that no end is scheduled or no announcer is called —
does not hold: that is an internal call, not the criterion's *Then* (D-09), and the thing
that would announce does not exist in this slice.

Route, the Team Lead's: FLOW-013 (7) is carried by the task that builds the announcement
(T-023), or T-022 carries the announcement it constrains; either way the entry's and
T-023's carried-elsewhere lines are brought into step, and T-022 returns to gate 1.
